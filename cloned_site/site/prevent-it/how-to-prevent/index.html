How to Prevent

The fight against hazing is everyone's responsibility. These tips help students and community members know how to recognize, respond to, and proactively prevent hazing.

Speak Up

The first step to ending hazing is to identify it and make your voice heard. Ask specific, clarifying questions if you see something that could be considered hazing.

Questions you can ask

“I noticed you missed last night's meeting. You look tired and in pain—is everything ok?”

“I saw you wearing a funny outfit in class today. Did someone force you to wear that?”

“You haven't turned in your homework and your grades dropped. What's going on?”

“Why do you have so many bruises on you? Did someone hit you?”

“How did that symbol appear on your skin? Were you branded?”

“We haven't seen you in a few weeks. Are you being forced to self-isolate?”

Remember, these conversations are always situational and contextual. You're more likely to get an honest response if the person trusts you. Even if they insist they aren't being hazed, hazing occurs with and without the victim's consent.
