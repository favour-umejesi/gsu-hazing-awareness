Parents & Family

You're part of the prevention team

It's important to speak with your student about hazing, because it can occur within any student group or organization. Consistent communication is one of the most powerful tools to prevent it.

Start the Talk

Start the conversation

Speak respectfully

Your student respects your judgment. A non-judgmental tone keeps the door open.

Be persistent

Move the conversation forward. Check in regularly, not just once.

Understand the pull

Belonging matters. Acknowledge the desire to fit in, and be there before harm happens.

Report right away

If you suspect hazing, make a report. You don't need proof to raise a concern.

Questions to ask your student

  • Are you in the process of joining an organization or team?
  • How much time do you spend on new-member activities?
  • Are you having trouble keeping up with academics?
  • Are you being deprived of anything, such as sleep?
  • What would you tell me about a hazing incident?
  • Do you feel safe and respected in your organization?
Trust Your Instincts

Warning signs to watch for

No single sign means hazing is happening, but several together are worth a closer conversation.

Sudden secrecy

Your student stops sharing details about meetings or activities.

Unexplained injuries

Bruises, exhaustion, or changes in how they look or move.

Becoming isolated

Pulling away from family, friends, or their usual support.

Mood or sleep changes

New anxiety, irritability, or disrupted sleeping and eating habits.

Drop in grades

Activities consuming all their time and hurting coursework.

Odd hours & errands

Always being asked to run errands or perform tasks at all hours.
