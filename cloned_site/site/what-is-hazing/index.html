Know It When You See It

What Is Hazing?

Hazing takes many forms and can be tricky to identify. It often hides behind tradition, secrecy, and the idea of "earning" your place. Learn the signs so you'll recognize it.

Hazing is any activity expected of someone joining or participating in a group that humiliates, degrades, abuses, or endangers them, regardless of their willingness to participate.

GSU Code of Student Conduct & StopHazing.org

Understanding Severity

The Spectrum of Hazing, it's all hazing

Hazing exists on a spectrum, from subtle to severe. Behaviors at the "milder" end are easy to excuse, but they are exactly what escalate over time. Every level is still hazing.

Intimidation

Lower Severity
  • Lies and purposeful deception
  • Assigning demerits or punishment
  • Forced silence
  • Social isolation of new members
  • Use of demeaning names
  • Expecting members to carry certain items at all times

Harassment

Moderate Severity
  • Verbal abuse
  • Threats or implied threats
  • Asking members to wear humiliating clothing
  • Forced servitude
  • "Skit nights" with humiliating acts
  • Sleep deprivation
  • Requiring new members to learn songs or chants

Violence

High Severity
  • Forced or coerced use of food, alcohol, or other drugs
  • Beating, paddling, or any form of physical assault
  • Branding
  • Staged kidnappings
  • Sexual assault
  • Forced consumption of vile substances
All of This Is Hazing

Examples of hazing

Drawn directly from the GSU Code of Student Conduct. These actions qualify as hazing regardless of a person's willingness to participate.

Substances & alcohol

  • Forced or coerced consumption of food, alcohol, drugs, or any other substance
  • Placement of undesirable substances on or in the body
  • Deprivation of food or water

Physical strain

  • Unnecessary physical contact such as whipping, beating, paddling, or branding
  • Calisthenics such as sit-ups, push-ups, or wall sits
  • Unnecessary fatigue, physical activity, or sleep deprivation

Control & servitude

  • Servitude for personal benefit such as driving, cooking, cleaning, or running errands
  • Required periods of silence or forced wearing of inappropriate apparel
  • Lineups or events where individuals are yelled or cursed at
